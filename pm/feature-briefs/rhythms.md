# Rhythms — a loop in a life, its cadence, and the check-in that records what was found

> **jojobot** · kind: feature-brief · boot: on-demand · verified: 2026-09-03

**A rhythm is a thing that comes round again** — watering a fern, paying a bill each month, changing an air filter. jojobot holds the loop itself, and each turn of it is an event recorded against it.

## A loop is its own thing, and it always has a parent

**A rhythm is an entity, not a key hung on the thing it is about.** Marge's fern is a `thing`; watering it is a rhythm whose parent is the fern. **The parent answers whose job it is.**

**It is its own entity so it can carry its own events.** Keys folded onto the fern could hold *when it was last watered* and nothing else — no history of what each turn found, no way to ask when the loop itself changed. A loop with a life has one.

## A cadence is always time

**How often is a number of days. It is never a distance, a count of uses, or a condition.** A bike serviced every 500 kilometres is a real thing somebody wants; it is not a rhythm, because a rhythm's whole arithmetic is *a day plus a span of days*.

**A measurement rides on the check-in instead.** The loop asks, and the turn records what was found. So the grease trap in the Belcher kitchen is checked monthly, and each check-in carries how full it was. **The rhythm holds the schedule; the check-in holds the world.**

## Three outcomes, and the difference is whether the cycle is consumed

**A turn either RAN, was SKIPPED, or was SNOOZED. A refusal is not a run.**

* **Ran** — it happened. The schedule moves.
* **Skipped** — it did not happen and the cycle advances anyway. The schedule moves exactly as a run would, and only the outcome tells them apart.
* **Snoozed** — not now, and not until a named day. The contact is recorded, **the schedule does not move**, and the loop is not owed again before the day the snooze names. On that day it comes back and is acted on again. **A snooze always names its day**, because *not now* with no day is a promise to come back that nothing can keep — Marge away until the 20th means the fern is not due before the 20th, and is due on it.

## Two dates, and the reason is the third outcome

**A rhythm keeps the day it was last checked in on, and the day the next cycle counts from.** They are usually the same day and they diverge in three situations: a late check-in that counts from the day it was *due* rather than the day it happened, a skipped cycle, and a snooze. **A snooze also holds the day it lasts until**, and the loop falls due on the later of that day and its own; the next run or skip spends it.

⛔️ **One date cannot carry this.** A single *last ran* key is written by a skipped turn — so it would say the loop ran on a day the record explicitly says it did not. **A key that lies is worse than a key that is missing**, because nothing downstream can tell.

**Which of the two dates a late check-in advances from is the loop's own to declare**, because a monthly bill and a fern want opposite answers: a bill paid three days late is still that month's bill, and a fern watered three days late resets the clock from the watering.

## A loop that arrives already part-way through

**Life hands you loops that were running before jojobot heard about them.** The chain was last done in December. The filter was changed in the spring.

**Such a loop is opened the same way an ordinary cycle is closed: check in on it, dated the day it last ran, and jojobot works the schedule out from there.** Nobody types the day the cycle counts from — **it is jojobot's arithmetic on every cycle including the first**, which is what makes the whole schedule rest on something derived rather than on a number somebody chose and nobody can check.

**On that first cycle the late-check-in policy has nothing to choose between**, because no cycle has ever fallen due. The basis is the check-in's own day under either answer.

⛔️ **A snooze cannot open a loop.** A snooze is defined as the outcome that leaves a schedule where it was, and there is no schedule yet to leave — so it refuses, and names the outcomes that do open one. **A run and a skip both open it**, because a skip already advances a schedule exactly as a run does and only the record tells them apart.

## Asking what has gone quiet

**The overdue question takes the day it is asked about. It never reads a clock.** So the same question asked twice about the same day gives the same answer, and a session can ask about any day at all.

⚠️ **A loop whose schedule cannot be read comes back OVERDUE, carrying its fields, rather than vanishing.** A loop nobody can schedule is exactly the one somebody needs to see. Silence would be the worst possible answer, because it is indistinguishable from *nothing is due*.

## What it is not

**Not a nag.** A rhythm is offered, never run on somebody's behalf. jojobot surfaces what has gone quiet and a person decides.

**Not a task.** A task is done once and leaves. A loop has no end state, and putting one on a board as a task means closing it and recreating it forever.

**Not a judgement.** jojobot performs no inference, so *a while ago* is a declared number of days compared against a date. **A loop with no cadence written down is still a loop** — it is simply one nothing can say is late.

**Not a job that fires.** Nothing sweeps, nothing wakes up, nothing sends. The loop is read when somebody asks.

**Not a log of everything that happened to the parent.** A servicing rhythm hangs off Milhouse's bike and records servicing. What the bike *is* stays on the bike.
