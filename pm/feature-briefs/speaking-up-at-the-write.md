# Speaking up at the write — one suggestion, rarely

> **jojobot** · kind: feature-brief · boot: on-demand

**When a write lands, jojobot may add one suggestion to its receipt: the single most useful thing the writer did not ask about, said only when jojobot is confident, and never twice in a session.**

## Why the write

**The writer knows, at the moment of writing, what the thing is and why it matters.** A minute later that knowledge is gone, and fixing a misfiled claim, a duplicate or a bloated record costs a cleanup by somebody who no longer knows. So the cheapest moment to say *this already exists under another name* or *this belongs over there* is the receipt of the write that is about to cause it.

**The receipt is already read.** A writer checks that its write landed, so a line there is seen without a new verb, a new call or a new habit.

## Rationed, because every line is paid for

**At most one suggestion per write.** When several apply they are ranked and only the first is said; the rest are dropped, not queued. A receipt that lists everything it noticed is a receipt the writer learns to skip, and then the one that mattered is skipped too.

**Only with high confidence, and silent otherwise.** A false suggestion costs the writer a read and costs jojobot its credibility. Silence is the common answer and the right one.

**Never the same suggestion twice in one session.** The session's ledger of what it has been taught and shown decides; a compaction report clears it, so a writer that forgot is told again.

**It fits inside the answer's budget** and is the first thing dropped when the answer is full.

## It never decides

**The write has always landed.** A suggestion names what it noticed and the call that acts on it, and the writer chooses. Nothing is moved, merged, renamed or refused on the writer's behalf: a refusal is a different feature (a container's ceiling, the creation guard), and the two never share a sentence.

## The angles it speaks from

These are the kinds of thing worth one line at a write. They are listed so the feature can grow along them; each earns its place only when a paid room or real use shows a writer acting wrongly without it.

- **A neighbour: the same thing under another name.** By meaning, where the words share no letters (*vehicle* and *car*): a thesaurus that states the relation, and a static embedding beside it, the writer told which one proposed it, because an embedding alone also brings opposites close. By spelling, where a name was typed twice slightly differently: normalise, then edit distance and trigram, with a phonetic fallback. By nickname (*Bart* for *Bartholomew*): a curated table, a lookup and nothing more.
- **The wrong subject.** The claim says another known thing's whole name, so it may belong there.
- **A feature that fits what was just written.** A long reusable text that would be a document; a second status record on a thread where the first could be rewritten; a correction posted to a box whose original is still unread, where replacing it would spare the reader; a skill that covers the act and was never read.
- **What the write unlocked or lost.** A key the thing's kind declares and the thing still lacks; a near miss of a key that would make a thing fall due.
- **A container close to its ceiling,** said before the write that would be refused.

## How it is judged

**It fires rarely and is right when it does.** A design that cannot say when it stays quiet is not finished. The measure is a paid room where a writer meets each angle cold: the suggestion should change what the writer does next, and a room with no trap should finish without one.

## What it is not

- **Not a refusal.** It never blocks or delays a write.
- **Not a list for the operator.** Possible duplicates and misfilings are never gathered into a page or a report on the operator's surfaces; tidying afterwards is defrag's work.
- **Not a classifier.** It never names a group (*these are about cycling*) or infers what the writer meant; it compares what was written with what is held.
- **Not search ranking.** Meaning-based matching is not added to every read; at most it answers an empty search with *did you mean*.
- **Not a nag.** No reminder repeats, and nothing is said about verbs a session has never touched.
