# What is owed and late — one read, whatever carries the due moment

> **jojobot** · kind: feature-brief · boot: on-demand · verified: 2026-09-11

**One question: what has a due moment that has passed, as of the day I name.** Not *which loops are overdue* — **what.** A loop gone quiet and a promise nobody delivered are the same question with two carriers, and they come back in one answer.

## Why it is one read rather than one per carrier

**The question was never about any particular kind of thing.** Something is owed and it is late; what kind of thing it happens to be changes what a reader should *do*, not what was asked.

⛔️ **Two reads answering one question is how a session learns two vocabularies.** It reaches for whichever it happened to meet first, gets a true answer to half the question, and cannot tell that the other half exists. **Generalising after a second carrier arrives means either forking the read or retrofitting it under pressure**, which is why the shape comes first and the carriers land into it.

## A thing appears here by CARRYING a due moment

**Carrying the key is the whole of qualifying.** Nothing is declared on the thing, and no list of the kinds that count sits inside the read. A recurring loop, an undelivered promise and something with an expiry all arrive the same way — and so does a kind nobody has invented yet.

**The read never holds the arithmetic**, and the carrier never holds code of its own either. What this question does is written once, here, for everything that qualifies.

**A due moment is STORED on the thing, never worked out while the question is being answered.** A one-off's is a day somebody set. A recurring loop's is a cadence after the date its cycle counts from — **jojobot does that arithmetic once, when a check-in is recorded, and keeps the answer on the thing as an ordinary key.** ⚠️ **A check-in advances that date only when it CONSUMES the cycle: one that ran does, one that was skipped does, and a snoozed one deliberately does not — so *the last check-in plus the cadence* is the wrong sum and would quietly move a snoozed loop out of the answer.** So the key reads the same whoever put it there, and the question never has to know which kind of thing it is looking at.

⛔️ **Nothing scans every thing in the store to work out which ones matter today.** A read that recomputes per object gets slower for ever while the answer it produces stays the same size, and the cost lands on every session rather than on the one write that could have paid it. **The stored key is what makes this a lookup instead of a sweep.**

⭐ **Once a thing is in hand, anything else is fair game.** The constraint is on what it costs to FIND the relevant things, not on what a reader may do with one it already has.

⭐ **That is the whole generalisation.** The moment one carrier's arithmetic lives inside the read, every future carrier is a change to the read — and the read is the thing every session depends on.

**The proof that it generalised is not that the code looks general.** It is that a carrier the read has never seen appears in the answer without the read changing.

## The day is always given, never taken from a clock

**The caller names the day it is asking about.** So the same question about the same day always returns the same answer, a session can ask about next Tuesday, and nothing in the answer depends on when it happened to run.

**A session supplies its timezone**, and what counts as *today* resolves against that. Two sessions in different zones legitimately disagree about what today is for one stored claim; the surface says so plainly, because an agent that meets that undocumented reads it as a fault and works around it.

## Two silences, and only one of them is correct

⚠️ **A thing whose schedule cannot be read comes back OVERDUE, carrying its fields.** The thing nobody can schedule is exactly the thing somebody needs to see, and dropping it is indistinguishable from *nothing is due*.

**A thing that carries no due moment at all is simply absent.** It was never owed. Those two cases look alike from a distance and the answer must not collapse them.

## The answer says what each thing is

**A loop gone quiet and an undelivered promise want different responses**, so each item names its own kind. A reader that cannot tell them apart either treats everything as a loop or asks a second question per item.

**The answer is bounded, and whatever did not fit is named with the call that returns it.** A count of what was held back is what separates a small answer from a lossy one.

⁉️ **What decides the ORDER is not settled, and it is the feature rather than a finishing touch.** A budget filled greedily by a bad order is a dump with a limit. **Which has gone quiet longest** is the shape a person already asks by hand, and it is the strongest candidate rather than the answer.

## 🚨 Being shown discharges nothing

**Nothing about appearing in this answer changes any record.** A bill does not become paid because it was put in front of an agent; it becomes paid because somebody recorded that it was paid.

**This is a read. Nothing fires** — no sweep, no job, no reminder, no clock. **The read is a question somebody asks, and asking it has no effect.**

⭐ **That is what makes the answer safe to surface aggressively.** A read that could discharge things would have to be cautious about what it showed; one that cannot is free to show everything relevant.

## What it is not

**Not a nag list.** It is a question with an answer, not a queue that accumulates until somebody dismisses each item.

**Not a scheduler and not a job.** Nothing runs on its own behalf.

**Not a task queue.** What is owed is a property of things that already exist for their own reasons, not a separate list of work.

**Not a judgement about importance.** Late is a date comparison. Whether something matters is a mind's call, and jojobot performs no inference.

**Not a place to record that something was done.** That happens on the thing itself, and this read simply stops returning it.
